\begin{abstract}
\noindent In machine learning for systems, a gap between a deployed heuristic and a future-seeing oracle is routinely treated as an opportunity for a learned model to capture. This paper tests that inference before any model is trained, using cache prefetching (loading data just before it is needed) as the demonstration domain. The test is a two-stage procedure this paper proposes and names \textbf{Opportunity Certification}. The first question is how much of the gap is real, since much of a raw oracle advantage comes from weak baselines, extra bandwidth, and objects no deployable predictor could foresee. The first stage, a measurement procedure called the \textbf{Instrument}, removes those three: it compares an adversarially tuned baseline against a future-seeing policy held to the same bandwidth and limited to objects a past-trained predictor could have named. Across twelve production traces the apparent opportunity shrinks by 14--87\%, and a statistically significant gap survives on only three of them, worth 12.7--19.7 hit-rate points. A surviving gap is not yet a capturable one, so the second question is whether any deployable policy could realize it. To answer it, the second stage, the \textbf{Necessity Ladder}, grants an oracle one perfect capability at a time: if even a perfect capability cannot close the gap, no learned model improving only that capability can. Each capability falls short: perfect arbitration adds under one hit-rate point, immediate fetching with a flawless forecaster recovers at most 6\% of the gap, and a learned timing model runs 26--40 hit-rate points below the baseline despite ranking future reuse accurately. The cause is \textbf{endogenous slack}: a policy's own mistimed fetches evict useful objects and shorten the lifetime of the very targets it aims at, so that an object's chance of surviving a hundred requests falls from near 1.0 to 0.05. A signal can therefore be ranked accurately yet never fetched in time. Even a policy designed specifically to escape this feedback loop fails, because the extra signal it adds is redundant with the predictor's own confidence. Applied unchanged to key-value cache warming in language-model serving, the procedure returns a different answer, no exploitable gap at all, because a tuned lookup table already sits on the physically reachable ceiling. Applied to learned indexes it instead returns a build, because there the oracle's advantage rests on the data rather than the future and a deployable model captures it. Four settings yield four pre-registered verdicts, from no corridor to a trap to a build, each reached before a model is trained. That certification procedure, not any single number, is the contribution.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A recurring pattern in machine learning for systems is that a measured gap between a deployed heuristic and an oracle is treated as a mandate to train a model that closes it. This paper takes the position that the inference from \emph{an opportunity exists} to \emph{learning can capture it} should itself be tested, and tested before a model is trained, because the opportunity is frequently mismeasured and, even when it is real, may rest on information that no deployable model possesses. The position is general; it is demonstrated here in cache prefetching, where both failure modes occur, and then instantiated in two further domains.

The prevailing yardsticks invite that mismeasurement. The gap to Belady's optimum \citep{belady} ignores prefetching, and its prefetch-aware repair \citep{demandmin} shows the optimum itself moves once prefetching is admitted. Oracle ceilings spend unbounded bandwidth against throttled or under-tuned baselines, and clairvoyant ceilings credit \emph{compulsory} misses (first references to never-seen objects) that no history-based policy can prefetch. A headroom figure built on these is inflated in at least three independent ways. In preliminary measurement, a $+42$-point synthetic headroom collapsed to $-0.02$ once the baseline was tuned across a predictor family and the oracle was held to the baseline's exact prefetch bandwidth, which fixes the two commitments the measurement enforces: a strongest-available decoupled baseline, and every ceiling charged the baseline's byte budget, under pre-registered thresholds and machine-checked invariants.

These failure modes motivate the paper's central proposal, a general procedure for deciding whether a learned component is warranted, named \textbf{Opportunity Certification}: an apparent oracle gap must pass two stages in sequence (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}). \emph{Stage 1, the Instrument,} certifies how much of the gap is \emph{real}, measuring it against the strongest available baseline at matched bandwidth over a reachable-only ceiling. \emph{Stage 2, the Necessity Ladder,} certifies whether the real gap is \emph{reachable} by a deployable policy, pricing each capability a learner could contribute with an oracle. The output is a certificate: no corridor, a trap, or build. The procedure is domain-general; cache prefetching is the primary instantiation, with LLM serving and learned indexes as two more.

\paragraph{Stage 1: the Instrument.} The first stage is a pre-registered, bandwidth-matched measurement whose object is the \textbf{learnable corridor}: the gap between the best decoupled predictor over a swept family and a ceiling restricted to objects a history-based predictor could name, both at one prefetch byte budget. Across 12 production traces it deflates by 14--87\% and survives on only 3, at $+12.7$ to $+19.7$ points, bootstrap-confirmed; a $2\times2$ study finds eviction and prefetching are \emph{substitutes}, not complements.

\paragraph{Stage 2: the Necessity Ladder.} The corridors Stage 1 certifies raise the second question: can any deployable policy capture them? Rather than train a scheduler and read its failure, the Ladder prices each capability one could contribute, arbitration, coverage, and timing, with an oracle, one at a time, before any model is trained (Figure~\ref{fig:ladder}): if an oracle holding one perfect capability cannot recover the corridor, no learner improving only that capability can. The verdict is a triple negative, explained by one mechanism, \textbf{endogenous slack}, and summarized as a distinction headroom studies do not draw, a signal can be \emph{rank-learnable} without being \emph{placeable}.

\paragraph{Instantiating the pipeline elsewhere.} Because Opportunity Certification is a general procedure, it is applied unchanged beyond object caching: to key-value cache warming in LLM serving, where it returns a third verdict of no corridor because a tuned table already sits on the reachable ceiling, and to learned indexes, where it returns a \textbf{build}, a corridor a deployable model captures because the oracle's advantage rests on the key distribution rather than the future. Whether that advantage is reachable is what separates a build from a trap.

\paragraph{Contributions.} (i) \textbf{Opportunity Certification}, a two-stage, domain-general procedure for deciding whether a learned component is warranted, with pre-registered thresholds and machine-checkable invariants; (ii) Stage 1, the Instrument, and a deflationary measurement across 12 traces (14--87\% phantom) plus the substitutes result and a taxonomy of four inflation mechanisms; (iii) Stage 2, the Necessity Ladder, and its threefold negative, showing the surviving corridor is reachable only with foresight; (iv) the endogenous-slack mechanism separating rank-learnability from placement; (v) a cross-domain instantiation (LLM serving and learned indexes) showing the procedure discriminates rather than merely deflates, green-lighting a learned component exactly where the oracle's advantage is reachable.

\section{Related Work}
Four lines of work bound or pursue cache-prefetch gains, and each leaves a different part of the honest-headroom question open; the specific gap this paper closes is named against each.

\textbf{Optimal replacement and prefetch-aware repairs.} Belady's MIN \citep{belady} is optimal for demand caching without prefetching. \citet{demandmin} showed that under prefetching MIN no longer minimizes demand misses and that ``the optimum'' is bandwidth-relative for uniform hardware lines. This work transports that insight to variable-size object caches and adds the reachability split: it bounds what a history-based policy could do, not what an omniscient one could. \citet{cao} gave near-optimal integrated prefetching-plus-caching under clairvoyance, uniform sizes, and a latency model, none of which holds for CDN/KV objects, so ceilings here are measured empirically per trace.

\textbf{Learned caching.} LRB \citep{lrb} imitates a relaxed Belady oracle for CDN eviction; Cold-RL \citep{coldrl}, DEAP \citep{deap}, DeePref \citep{deepref}, and LightCacheRL \citep{lightcache} learn eviction and/or prefetching; Pythia \citep{pythia} tunes hardware prefetching with online RL; \citet{jointhw} share representations across replacement and prefetching. \textbf{Baleen} \citep{baleen} is the nearest neighbor: it learns flash admission and prefetching against an OPT-derived episodes bound, but does not measure a corridor at matched bandwidth nor split it by reachability, so its target inherits the phantom component priced out here. To current knowledge no prior work constructs a pre-registered, bandwidth-matched, learnability-split headroom measurement for object caches, nor gates a learned component on an oracle decomposition of necessity.

\textbf{LLM serving caches and learned indexes.} Mooncake \citep{mooncake} disaggregates prefill and decode around a multi-tier KV cache and released production traces; PagedAttention \citep{vllm} and CacheGen \citep{cachegen} established and compressed prefix-shared KV caching. That literature reports achieved hit rates; Part III instead measures headroom. Learned indexes \citep{kraska,sosd} are the positive foil: a corridor the same procedure certifies as reachable and captured.

\textbf{Evaluation rigor as a contribution.} \citet{sober} showed claimed language-model reasoning gains shrink under variance-aware protocols; this paper is the caching analog, adding a capturability test before any learning is attempted. S3-FIFO \citep{s3fifo} is the fixed evictor throughout, so every corridor is attributable to prefetch scheduling; the substitutes result justifies freezing it.

\section{Stage 1: The Instrument}
The Instrument, the first certification stage, is one deterministic replay in which four arms share a single prefetch byte budget; the corridor is the gap between two of them, and every degree of freedom that could inflate it is fixed before any real-trace run.

\textbf{Setup.} Every arm is measured by replaying the same production trace through the same cache, so that any difference between arms is due to the prefetcher alone. A run uses a two-million-request prefix of a trace, a cache sized at 1\% of that trace's footprint, a 5\% warmup that is excluded from scoring, and the request-weighted hit ratio as the score. The evictor is fixed to S3-FIFO, a strong non-learned policy, throughout. Predictors are \emph{decoupled} from the future: they read only past requests, train on the first half of the trace, and are then frozen. A prefetched object that is later requested counts as a hit, one that is evicted before use counts as waste, and every prefetched byte counts as traffic; this accounting is unit-tested.

\textbf{The four arms.} All four are held to the tuned baseline's measured byte rate, and two of them bracket the corridor. \emph{BASE} is S3-FIFO with no prefetch; its origin traffic is the denominator everything else is measured against. \emph{TUNED BASELINE} is the best non-learned system that can be built against the corridor's own interest: the highest hit ratio over $\{$Markov-1, Markov-2, Markov-3, LSTM$\}$ across all confidence thresholds and fan-outs, subject to at most $1.15\times$ BASE traffic (a cap set slightly loose so the baseline is never rejected on rounding, which errs against the corridor). \emph{GROSS CEILING} is a clairvoyant prefetcher, one allowed to see the future and fetch the soonest-needed uncached objects, metered by a token bucket (a limiter that permits a fixed number of prefetch bytes per request) to the baseline's byte rate; it is reported only as a diagnostic, because it also fetches objects no real predictor could know. \emph{WARM CEILING}, the primary arm, is that same clairvoyant restricted to objects seen during training, at the same budget. Because the budget is fixed, whatever the gross oracle would waste on unreachable objects is re-spent on reachable ones, which makes the warm ceiling the honest upper bound for a history-based scheduler: perfect timing and realistic knowledge at equal cost.

\textbf{Why this interval, and for whom.} The learnable corridor is the gap between these two particular arms, and the choice of endpoints is what makes it a measurement rather than an arbitrary difference. The lower endpoint is the tuned baseline, not LRU or an untuned default, because headroom is meaningful only above the strongest system one would deploy without new ideas; a weaker lower endpoint measures baseline weakness rather than opportunity. The upper endpoint is the warm ceiling, not the gross clairvoyant ceiling or Belady, because the quantity of interest is what a \emph{history-based} predictor could reach at equal budget: the gross ceiling credits compulsory-miss elimination that no history-based policy can perform, and Belady scores eviction rather than prefetch and at unmatched bandwidth. The interval between the tightest deployable lower bound and the tightest history-reachable upper bound, both at one byte rate, therefore isolates precisely the headroom available to a history-based learned scheduler, which is the class this paper concerns. It is a conservative measure for that class rather than a universal one: content-aware or online-updating predictors lie outside it and would only widen it, as the scope section notes.

\textbf{Why the warm ceiling, not subtraction.} The shortcut ``learnable $\approx$ gross corridor $-$ cold slice'' is wrong in both directions. It over-penalizes because the oracle's cold prefetches consumed budget a warm-only policy would redirect (on Wikipedia, 27\% of the gross oracle's useful prefetches were cold; redirected, that budget buys $+5.4$ warm points). It can under-penalize via eviction coupling. Measured here, subtraction misstates the corridor by up to 5.4 points, enough to flip a verdict. Reachability must be priced \emph{inside} the replay, at the budget. An invariant enforces it: the bar and the warm ceiling each record exactly zero cold prefetch hits by construction. (A first ``cold'' definition, ``not yet requested at issue,'' violated this: the bar scored 442{,}188 ``cold'' hits where the out-of-vocabulary definition gives zero. The invariant caught it.)

\textbf{Pre-registration.} The verdict bar, learnable corridor $\ge 8$ hit-rate points \emph{and} Pareto dominance at the warm ceiling, was fixed before any real-trace run. Baselines escalate in pre-registered stages (coarse Markov-1/2, then fine-$\tau$ plus Markov-3, then LSTM), and a LIVE verdict is provisional until it survives the strongest stage. The escalation only raises the bar, so survival is meaningful and death final.

\section{Results: Measurement}
Two questions decide Part~I: how large is the honestly measured corridor, and where does the inflated part of the gross number go? The first is answered per trace, the second by a warm/cold decomposition.

\textbf{Which gross corridors survive.} Table~\ref{tab:gatea} reports Gate A, and three points stand out. First, the tuned baseline is decisive: \texttt{cluster26} falls below the threshold once the baseline is escalated. Second, the LSTM never recovers a surviving corridor, so the survivors are not artifacts of a weak predictor. Third, survival is not a property of the workload family, since the block traces yield both $+10.08$ and $-3.81$ and the key-value traces yield both $+25.72$ and $-1.18$.

\textbf{The learnable corridor: 14--87\% is phantom.} Table~\ref{tab:learnable} decomposes the six gross-live traces. The three CDN/block traces with the largest gross corridors lose 51--87\% to the cold slice, because high object churn spends the clairvoyant budget on compulsory misses; the two low-churn KV traces lose only 14--20\%. Wikipedia is the instructive middle case: 52\% phantom, yet enough exploitable warm reuse that the honest corridor still clears the threshold, but only once the warm ceiling re-spends the cold budget (naive subtraction leaves it at $+7.27$, a spurious elimination). Per-request hit indicators for bar and warm ceiling are recorded on the same post-warmup requests; the corridor is the mean paired difference, resampled as 1{,}000 contiguous blocks, 10{,}000 resamples (block, not i.i.d., because hits are autocorrelated through cache state). All three surviving lower bounds clear the 8-point threshold. (\texttt{cluster53}'s decomposition uses its Markov baseline; against its higher LSTM baseline, with the ceiling still handicapped to the smaller Markov budget, the corridor is $\ge +18.4$, so the verdict is unchanged.)

\textbf{Four inflation mechanisms, each caught on the record.} The deflation comes from four distinct mechanisms, each caught by a named component of the Instrument and each first exposed on the authors' own results. An \emph{under-tuned baseline} inflated a synthetic corridor to $+42$ points, which family-tuning and bandwidth-matching reduced to $-0.02$, and the same correction later moved \texttt{cluster26} from $+12.5$ to below the threshold. A \emph{bandwidth mismatch} let a synthetic oracle spend $1.27\times$ the baseline's traffic until the rate limiter removed the advantage. A \emph{traffic-bought corridor} on \texttt{cluster26} spent $1.16\times$ against the baseline's $1.09\times$, which the Pareto check exposed. And \emph{cold conflation} inflated \texttt{meta\_rprn} from $+32.6$ gross to $+4.3$ learnable, which the warm restriction removed; an earlier definition of ``cold'' even credited the baseline with 442{,}188 impossible cold hits before the out-of-vocabulary definition set that count to zero. Part II adds five further self-caught errors before any conclusion is drawn, two of them in the oracle arms themselves.

\textbf{Which corridors survive, and why.} The separation follows a two-factor pattern. Where no corridor survives, the tuned baseline already reaches the warm ceiling (\texttt{meta\_reag} 0.754 versus 0.791; \texttt{msr\_hm\_0} 0.838 versus 0.887), so whatever remains of the gross corridor is compulsory-cold and unreachable by construction. Where a corridor survives, there is reusable, in-vocabulary content that the tuned predictor could serve at the same budget but fails to schedule (Wikipedia baseline 0.553 versus warm ceiling 0.680). The learnable corridor is therefore exactly the quantity a learned scheduler would need to target.

\textbf{Eviction and prefetching are substitutes.} If better eviction and better prefetching fixed different misses, a joint policy could exploit the coupling. A $2\times2$ of $\{$S3-FIFO, Belady$\}\times\{$no prefetch, clairvoyant@matched-BW$\}$ measures the interaction (eviction gain under oracle prefetch minus eviction gain without). Six traces, all negative: \texttt{msr\_proj\_0} $-1.34$, \texttt{msr\_hm\_0} $-2.31$, wiki $-3.88$, \texttt{cluster53} $-6.66$, \texttt{cluster50} $-7.83$, \texttt{meta\_rprn} $-7.94$. Better prefetching reduces what better eviction can add on every trace measured; the decoupled design is therefore the indicated one, which justifies holding S3-FIFO fixed throughout Part II.

\textbf{Robustness to cache size and byte weighting.} The 1\% operating point above is one slice of a broader pattern. Re-running the Instrument at cache sizes of 0.1\%, 1\%, and 10\% of each trace's footprint (Table~\ref{tab:cachesweep}) shows the learnable corridor shrinking monotonically as the cache grows, because a larger cache leaves fewer additional misses for a prefetcher to convert: on Wikipedia it falls from $+15.2$ to $+5.1$ points, and survival becomes a property of the operating point (two further traces clear the bar at 0.1\%, none at 10\%). Byte-weighting deflates the corridor a second time, often below zero where the request-weighted figure is large (\texttt{meta\_reag} reads $+26.7$ points by request but $+4.9$ by byte), which identifies the surviving opportunity as a request-weighted, small-object effect.

\section{Stage 2: The Necessity Ladder}
The three surviving corridors naturally invite a learned scheduler. Instead of assuming one would work, the Necessity Ladder tests the assumption through a sequence of questions, where each rung is a pre-registered, replay-only experiment that isolates a single capability with an oracle and measures its contribution before any model is trained (Figure~\ref{fig:ladder}, Table~\ref{tab:decomp}). Every arm holds S3-FIFO fixed, respects the baseline's byte budget through the same token bucket, and reports paired block-bootstrap confidence intervals. The oracle arms exist only to bound what is achievable, and are never reported as deployable results.

\begin{figure}[t]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth, height=10cm]{Figures/ladder.pdf}
\caption{The Necessity Ladder as run (wiki / cluster50). Green rungs are clairvoyant checks that certify the corridor reachable in principle (F1 timing, F5 coverage); red rungs are negatives. Each rung is a pre-registered oracle decomposition pricing one ingredient by replay, ending with CGP, a policy built specifically to escape the bottleneck.}
\label{fig:ladder}
\end{figure}

\textbf{F1: the corridor is a timing problem, in principle.} Giving an oracle perfect control of only the timing of each fetch, restricted to the objects the predictor could itself name, recovers 95.8\% of the learnable corridor on Wikipedia and 91.2\% on \texttt{cluster50}: the corridor is a question of \emph{when} to fetch, not of \emph{which} object to fetch. On \texttt{cluster53} the same oracle recovers only 7.3\%, so its corridor is a matter of object choice rather than timing; that trace leaves the capture study, and the results below concern the two traces where timing is the question.

\textbf{F4$'$: learned arbitration is unnecessary.} Reinforcement learning would help here only if choosing which candidates to fetch as a set beat choosing each one independently. Given identical value estimates and budget, an optimal joint chooser improves on a simple per-candidate threshold rule by only $+0.01$ points on Wikipedia and $-0.01$ on \texttt{cluster50}, far short of the pre-registered 2-point requirement; a control that gives both choosers the true values agrees, reading near zero. The choice is therefore separable, and a threshold rule suffices.

\textbf{F1-stream: timing what is offered loses.} Perfect timing applied only to the candidates the baseline actually emits, one per request, and dropping those never reused, lands \emph{below} the baseline: $-7.01$ $[-7.68,-6.33]$ on Wikipedia and $-10.82$ $[-11.70,-9.93]$ on \texttt{cluster50}. No scheduler can win by re-timing the candidates the baseline already names, so the corridor is a problem of \emph{coverage}, not of timing.

\textbf{F5: coverage plus clairvoyant timing reaches it.} Widening the emission (raising the fan-out to 32 and removing the confidence floor), then covering just before use only those reuses the wide predictor could name in time, clears the threshold: $+10.41$ $[8.74,12.08]$ on Wikipedia and $+11.42$ $[9.95,12.91]$ on \texttt{cluster50}. The corridor is therefore reachable in principle: breadth supplies the candidates, and perfectly timed insertion converts them.

\textbf{Policy 1: fetching at prediction time captures at most 6\%.} The natural causal version of F5 fetches each candidate as soon as it is predicted and holds it until use. A diagnostic arm gives this design a \emph{perfect} forecaster and F5's own selection rule, so the only difference from F5 is \emph{when} the fetch happens. It still loses 10 to 15 points to F5 in every configuration, capturing at most 6\% of the corridor and turning negative on \texttt{cluster50}. Because even a perfect forecaster fails when it fetches at prediction time, no better forecaster can rescue the design; the missing ingredient is just-in-time insertion, meaning placement immediately before use.

\textbf{F7: causal timing cannot place the insertion.} F7 keeps F5's clairvoyant coverage and selection and changes only when each object is fetched. The \texttt{oracle} arm fetches at the true next-use time and reproduces F5 ($+9.69$, $+11.69$; a passing construction check); the \texttt{model} arm fetches at a time predicted by a causal gap model from recency, frequency, and confidence. The model passes the usual learnability test (rank correlation $0.60$/$0.43$, and $0.92$/$0.81$ accuracy separating reused from never-reused objects) yet its arm lands far below the baseline, $-40.06$ $[-42.7,-37.4]$ on Wikipedia and $-26.01$ $[-27.9,-24.1]$ on \texttt{cluster50}: precision collapses to $0.05$ and it issues up to $1.5$M fetches against the baseline's $1.0$M. A pre-registered retrain on the wide stream raises rank correlation ($0.60{\to}0.68$) without improving capture; a better-fitting predictor places its fetches no better.

\textbf{The mechanism: endogenous slack.} Why does a model that ranks reuse timing well still fail to place its fetches? Under the baseline's light load a prefetched object almost always survives until use, its chance of still being cached one hundred requests later being essentially $1$, so a timing model would seem to have a wide window to aim at. That window, however, is set by the policy's own behaviour and not by the workload. A wide causal policy that mistimes issues far more fetches (its precision falls toward $0.05$), and the extra traffic evicts useful objects sooner, shrinking the very window each later fetch must hit: measured directly, the hundred-request survival probability falls from $1.0$ to about $0.05$, and the median time an object stays cached drops to a few tens of requests. The lesson is that \textbf{a signal can be rank-learnable without being placeable}: a learnability test certifies only that a signal orders objects correctly, whereas placement requires it to fall inside a window that the decision itself narrows. This pairing of a passed learnability test with a failed placement is, to current knowledge, the first direct demonstration that the gap between the two can be complete.

\textbf{The constructive escape, and why it also fails.} Endogenous slack suggests its own remedy: add coverage \emph{without} adding fetch volume. Corroboration-Gated Prefetch (CGP) is the simplest design of this kind. A wide predictor \emph{arms} candidates onto a byte-free watchlist, recording them without fetching or occupying the cache, and a tight predictor then \emph{fires} an actual fetch only when its own signal triggers, choosing among watchlisted candidates by confidence and staying within the baseline's exact byte budget. Coverage comes from the free watchlist while fetch volume stays at the baseline's, so the survival curve cannot collapse. CGP avoids every pitfall the ladder identified (jointness, re-timing offered candidates, fetching at prediction time, forecasting a time) and with perfect components reduces to F5. It nonetheless fails: $-7.60$ $[-8.1,-7.1]$ on Wikipedia and $-5.39$ $[-5.9,-4.9]$ on \texttt{cluster50}. The reason is specific. Raising the weight on the watchlist shifts the funded set from 43\% to 69\% watchlisted objects while its precision does not move (0.269 to 0.268), so gating on the watchlist adds only $+0.18$ and $-0.04$ points over an otherwise identical policy without it. The watchlist signal is statistically redundant with the predictor's own confidence: that an object was plausible over a long horizon adds nothing to the fact that it is likely to be requested next. No causal policy can widen coverage without also widening the low-precision volume that endogenous slack penalises, which is a stronger negative result than a simple failure, because the policy was built against the exact bottleneck and the bottleneck absorbed it.

\section{The Procedure Across Domains}
A measurement procedure is only as valuable as its ability to return different answers on different inputs, and the Instrument does. The same three arms and pre-registered bar were carried, unchanged in logic, to two settings beyond object caching: one returns a third distinct negative, the other a positive.

\textbf{LLM KV-cache serving (no corridor).} Applied unchanged to KV-cache warming in LLM serving on Mooncake production traces \citep{mooncake} (one hour each of conversation and tool/agent traffic; objects are 512-token KV blocks with hashed identities), warming a block into the HBM cache before its next use is the prefetch analog and the hit rate measures the prefill computation avoided, which is the time-to-first-token lever. Two adaptations keep the port honest: \emph{turn gating} (a request reads its whole prefix at once, so per-access prefetch would fake-convert whole chains at zero latency; every arm may warm only at request boundaries, making the corridor measure cross-turn warming), and a \emph{physical} reachability restriction (a KV block never computed cannot be warmed by any system, so the warm ceiling is the true ceiling). The baselines are inherited unchanged, and a capped-baseline control (the same baseline run under the ceiling's token bucket) separates the cost of the rate limit itself from any difference in scheduling quality.

The verdict is the third distinct outcome: \textbf{no corridor}. A tuned Markov table over block IDs already sits within noise of the physically reachable, bandwidth-matched clairvoyant ceiling; even like-for-like the margin is only $+2$--$3$ points against an 8-point gate (warm ceiling versus the capped baseline: conversation $+2.98$, tool/agent $+2.14$; versus the tuned baseline both are negative, $-0.56$ and $-1.87$). The reason is structural: prefix-chain reuse is near-deterministic (held-out next-request coverage 0.728 vs 0.008 for a popularity baseline), so the trivial policy is already the ceiling. The conversation trace's reuse \emph{timing} is learnable (think-time hazard $\rho{=}0.27$, AUC 0.81), but there is no corridor for that signal to capture; tool/agent has neither ($\rho{=}0.003$). A single replay therefore advises against training a learned KV-cache warmer for this workload.

\textbf{Learned indexes (a captured corridor).} The same three-arm procedure applies outside caching. In a static learned index the task is to locate a key in a sorted array: the tuned non-learned baseline is binary search at $\lceil\log_2 N\rceil$ probes, the oracle is a perfect index at one probe, and the deployable learned arm is a two-stage recursive model (RMI) trained on the keys alone, which predicts a position and finishes with a last-mile search over its own guaranteed error window \citep{kraska}. The decisive difference from caching is \emph{reachability}. The caching oracle's advantage rests on the future, which no deployable policy holds, so its corridor is a trap; an index oracle's advantage rests only on the key distribution, since a key's position is $N$ times its cumulative distribution and is learnable from the keys themselves. The corridor here is reachable, so the procedure should be able to certify it.

Table~\ref{tab:index} reports the corridor and what a deployable RMI captures, on two real key sources: the object identifiers of the caching traces above, and the SOSD learned-index benchmark of natural keys \citep{sosd}. The verdict is BUILD, and the same procedure discriminates. The learned index captures 56--86\% of the corridor on the more regular distributions (\texttt{books}, \texttt{meta\_rprn}, \texttt{osm\_cellids}) and clears the pre-registered bar, while it captures less on the distributions known to be hard for a piecewise-linear model (\texttt{fb} 41\%, and the structured Wikipedia identifiers 23\%). A correctness invariant, that every key lies inside the search window the model declares, holds on every run. This is the positive counterpart to the caching negatives: applied across settings the Instrument does not only deflate, it green-lights a learned component exactly where the oracle's advantage is reachable. Four settings, four verdicts spanning the range from no corridor to a captured one: no corridor on block storage and on LLM KV-cache serving, a corridor reachable only with foresight on CDN and key-value prefetching, and a captured corridor on learned indexes.

\section{Scope and Threats to Validity}
Five limits bound the claims, and naming them also fixes their direction: the measurement caveats point toward conservatism, the capture caveats toward a narrower but firmer negative result.

``Learnable'' is defined against frozen, history-based prediction (training-prefix vocabulary). An online-updating or content-aware predictor could reach cold objects and would \emph{raise} the surviving corridors, so the measurement verdicts are conservative in that direction. The capture verdict is not symmetric: F5 already grants the widest emission the family supports, and F7/CGP fail for timing and redundancy reasons a larger vocabulary does not touch; the F7 negative result was accepted only after the pre-registered wide-stream fallback. The capture negative result is bounded by its decomposition (Table~\ref{tab:decomp}); a design outside it (for example, modifying the evictor to protect warmed objects) is out of scope, though the substitutes result argues against it. The primary operating point is a 1\% cache with 2M-request prefixes; cache-size (0.1--10\%) and byte-weighted results are reported in Table~\ref{tab:cachesweep} and point the same way. Twitter traces are 1-in-10 samples, so absolute OHRs are not comparable to unsampled deployments, and LLM traces cover one hour of one provider. CIs cover within-trace variance only; every error in the baseline inflates corridors, so the negative results are conservative under further scrutiny.

\section{Conclusion}
Measured honestly against a family-tuned baseline at matched bandwidth, most celebrated prefetch headroom in caching is phantom: 14--87\% of gross corridors evaporate across 12 production traces. What survives is real but, priced by a ladder of pre-registered oracle decompositions, reachable only with foresight; every deployable ingredient a learned scheduler could contribute is shown insufficient, for one reason with a name, endogenous slack, and a general form, rank-learnable is not placeable. The value of Opportunity Certification is that it discriminates rather than merely deflates: across four settings it returns four verdicts, from no corridor (block storage, LLM KV serving) to a trap (CDN and key-value prefetching) to a build (learned indexes), with reachability the axis that separates them, each decided by replay before any model is trained. The procedure and all replay code are released, so the next celebrated headroom number can be checked in an afternoon.
