\begin{abstract}
\noindent In machine learning for systems, a measured gap between a deployed heuristic and an oracle is usually taken as an invitation to train a model that closes it. This paper contends that the passage from possibility to policy needs to be empirically shown, and shown before any model is trained; this paper shows that empirically in cache prefetching. Two artifacts support the claim. The \emph{Instrument} quantifies how much of the eligible prefetch headroom is actual, measuring an adversarially tuned baseline against a clairvoyant ceiling that is bandwidth-matched to it and limited to objects a history-based predictor could have inferred; across twelve production traces this takes corridors back by 14 to 87 percent and leaves only three with bootstrapped headroom of $+12.7$ to $+19.7$ hit-rate points. The \emph{Necessity Ladder} then asks whether that edge is capturable, giving an oracle one perfect ability at a time, arbitration, coverage, or timing: if a perfect copy of an ability cannot recover the corridor, no learned policy that can only enhance that ability can either. None does. Learned arbitration adds under one hit-rate point; dispatch at prediction time captures at most six percent of the corridor even with a perfect forecaster; and causal insertion timing lands 26 to 40 points below the baseline, with a better-fit forecaster (rank correlation raised from 0.60 to 0.68) capturing no more. A single mechanism explains it, \emph{endogenous slack}: a policy's own mistimed fetch volume collapses the cache-residence survival curve from 1.0 to 0.05 and so destroys the window its timing must hit, which is why a signal can be rank-learnable without being placeable, and why the surviving corridor is reachable only by clairvoyance. Applied unchanged to key-value cache warming in language-model serving, the Instrument returns a third verdict, no corridor at all, a clairvoyant margin of two to three points against the eight-point bar, because a tuned table already sits on the physical ceiling. The outcome is three workload families and three pre-registered verdicts on whether to build a learned scheduler, each reached before a model was trained.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A recurring pattern in machine learning for systems is that a measured gap between a deployed heuristic and an oracle is treated as a mandate to train a model that closes it. This paper takes the position that the inference from \emph{an opportunity exists} to \emph{learning can capture it} should itself be tested, and tested before a model is trained, because the opportunity is frequently mismeasured and, even when it is real, may rest on information that no deployable model possesses. The position is general; it is demonstrated here in the domain of cache prefetching, where the opportunity is the hit-rate a learned scheduler could add over a strong non-learned baseline, and where both failure modes are shown to occur.

The prevailing yardsticks in this domain invite exactly that mismeasurement. The gap to Belady's optimum \citep{belady} ignores prefetching, and its prefetch-aware repair \citep{demandmin} shows that the optimum itself moves once prefetching is admitted. Oracle-prefetcher ceilings spend unbounded bandwidth while the baselines they dwarf are throttled or under-tuned. And clairvoyant ceilings credit the elimination of \emph{compulsory} misses, first references to never-seen objects, which no history-based policy can prefetch. A headroom figure built on these is inflated in at least three independent ways.

It was observed, in preliminary measurement, that a headroom of $+42$ hit-rate points on a synthetic workload collapsed to $-0.02$ once the baseline was tuned across a predictor family and the oracle was constrained to the baseline's exact prefetch bandwidth. Observations of this kind fix the two commitments the measurement below enforces, that the baseline is the strongest decoupled system available and that every ceiling is charged the baseline's byte budget, and they recommend the discipline of pre-registered thresholds and machine-checked invariants that the rest of the paper follows.

The argument is carried by two artifacts, applied in sequence and named throughout. \textbf{The Instrument} measures how much of the apparent opportunity is genuine; \textbf{the Necessity Ladder} then asks whether the genuine opportunity is capturable. The Instrument answers \emph{how much is real}, the Necessity Ladder answers \emph{can learning reach it}, and together they convert a headroom number into a decision about whether a model is worth building at all.

\paragraph{The Instrument (Part I).} The first artifact is a pre-registered, bandwidth-matched measurement whose central object is the \textbf{learnable corridor}: the gap between a baseline that cannot be dismissed as under-tuned, the best decoupled predictor over a swept family, and a ceiling restricted to objects a history-based predictor could ever name, both held to one prefetch byte budget. The corridor is the headroom a history-based learned scheduler could add. Across 12 production traces it deflates by 14--87\% and survives on only 3, with corridors of $+12.7$ to $+19.7$ points, bootstrap-confirmed. A $2\times2$ interaction study finds eviction quality and prefetching are \emph{substitutes}, not complements.

\paragraph{The Necessity Ladder (Part II).} The corridors the Instrument certifies raise the question the second artifact answers: can any deployable policy capture them? Rather than train a scheduler and interpret its failure, the Necessity Ladder prices each capability such a scheduler could contribute, perfect arbitration, perfect coverage, and perfect timing, with an oracle, one capability at a time, before any model is trained (Figure~\ref{fig:ladder}). The governing logic is that if an oracle holding one perfect capability cannot recover the corridor, no learned policy that improves only that capability can either. The verdict is a triple negative that renders the surviving corridor reachable only by clairvoyance, is explained by a single mechanism, \textbf{endogenous slack}, and is summarized as a distinction headroom studies do not currently draw: a signal can be \emph{rank-learnable} without being \emph{placeable}.

\paragraph{A Second Domain (Part III).} Because the Instrument is a general procedure rather than a single-workload result, it is applied unchanged to a second, economically pressing domain, key-value cache warming in large-language-model serving. There it returns a third distinct verdict, no corridor at all, because a tuned association table already sits on the physically reachable ceiling. Three workload families thus receive three different pre-registered verdicts, each reached before a model is trained.

\paragraph{Contributions.} (i) a pre-registered iso-bandwidth instrument whose object is the learnable corridor, with machine-checkable invariants and a taxonomy of four inflation mechanisms; (ii) a deflationary measurement across 12 traces plus the substitutes result; (iii) the Necessity Ladder and its verdict, a triple kill that renders the surviving corridor clairvoyance-priced; (iv) the endogenous-slack mechanism separating rank-learnability from placement; (v) the first honest headroom measurement for LLM KV-cache warming, and with it a three-domain demonstration that the instrument discriminates rather than merely deflates. No prevalence is claimed. The claims are existence with confidence intervals, absence with mechanisms, unreachability by oracle decomposition, and a protocol others can apply.

\section{Related Work}
Four lines of work bound or chase cache-prefetch gains, and each leaves a different part of the honest-headroom question open; the specific gap this paper closes is named against each.

\textbf{Optimal replacement and prefetch-aware repairs.} Belady's MIN \citep{belady} is optimal for demand caching without prefetching. \citet{demandmin} showed that under prefetching MIN no longer minimizes demand misses and that a family of prefetch-aware optima trades demand misses against prefetch traffic, establishing that ``the optimum'' is bandwidth-relative for uniform hardware lines. This work transports that insight to variable-size object caches and adds the reachability split: it bounds what a history-based policy could do, not what an omniscient one could. \citet{cao} gave near-optimal integrated prefetching-plus-caching under clairvoyance, uniform sizes, and a latency model; none of the three holds for CDN/KV objects, so ceilings here are measured empirically per trace.

\textbf{Learned caching.} LRB \citep{lrb} imitates a relaxed Belady oracle for CDN eviction; Cold-RL \citep{coldrl}, DEAP \citep{deap}, DeePref \citep{deepref}, and LightCacheRL \citep{lightcache} learn eviction and/or prefetching; Pythia \citep{pythia} tunes hardware-prefetcher aggressiveness with online RL; \citet{jointhw} share representations between hardware replacement and prefetching. \textbf{Baleen} \citep{baleen} is the nearest neighbor: it learns flash admission and prefetching and reports against an OPT-derived episodes bound, but it does not measure a corridor at matched prefetch bandwidth nor split its bound by reachability, so its target inherits exactly the phantom component priced out here. To current knowledge no prior work constructs a pre-registered, bandwidth-matched, learnability-split headroom measurement for object caches, nor gates a learned component on an oracle decomposition of necessity.

\textbf{LLM serving caches.} Mooncake \citep{mooncake} disaggregates prefill and decode around a multi-tier KV cache and released production traces with hashed block identities; PagedAttention \citep{vllm} established paged, prefix-shared KV caching; CacheGen \citep{cachegen} streams and compresses KV state. That literature reports achieved hit rates; Part III supplies what it does not, a headroom measurement separating what a smarter warming policy \emph{could} add from what a tuned trivial policy already achieves under physical reachability and matched bandwidth.

\textbf{Evaluation rigor as a contribution.} \citet{sober} showed that claimed language-model reasoning gains shrink under standardized, variance-aware protocols. This paper is the caching analog, with one addition: it prices the corrected headroom's capturability before any learning is attempted. S3-FIFO \citep{s3fifo} is the fixed evictor throughout, so every corridor is attributable to prefetch scheduling rather than evictor weakness; the substitutes result justifies freezing it.

\section{The Instrument}
The Instrument is one deterministic replay in which four arms share a single prefetch byte budget; the corridor is the gap between two of them, and every degree of freedom that could inflate it is fixed before any real-trace run.

\textbf{Setup.} Deterministic replay over 2M-request prefixes of production traces, byte-capacity cache at 1\% of trace footprint, first 5\% of requests as warmup, request-weighted object hit ratio (OHR) as the primary metric. The evictor is S3-FIFO in every arm. Predictors are decoupled (they see the request stream, never the future), train on the first 50\% and are frozen; association tables store full confidence-ranked candidate lists so a sweep over threshold $\tau$ and degree $k$ costs one table build. Prefetched bytes are charged to origin traffic; a prefetched-then-requested object is a hit, a prefetched-then-evicted object is waste. This accounting is unit-tested.

\textbf{The four arms.} All four run at the tuned bar's measured byte rate, and two of them bracket the corridor. \emph{BASE}: S3-FIFO, no prefetch; its origin bytes are the traffic denominator. \emph{TUNED BAR}: the best decoupled system constructible against the corridor's own interest, $\max$ OHR over $\{$Markov-1, Markov-2, Markov-3 ($3{\to}2{\to}1$ backoff), LSTM$\}\times\tau\times k$ subject to origin traffic $\le 1.15\times$ BASE. The cap is 1.15, not 1.10, because the default config lands at $1.10\times$ and a tighter cap could reject it on float noise; the cap errs against the corridor. \emph{GROSS CEILING}: a clairvoyant prefetcher (soonest-future uncached objects) throttled by a token bucket to the bar's measured prefetch byte \emph{rate} (a rate, not a total: a total is spent greedily and then stops, which once made an oracle score below a heuristic). Reported as a diagnostic. \emph{WARM CEILING} (primary): the same clairvoyant restricted to training-vocabulary objects, same bucket. Because the bucket is unchanged, budget the gross oracle wastes on unreachable cold objects is \emph{re-spent} on warm ones. This is the honest upper bound for any history-based scheduler: perfect timing, realistic knowledge, equal budget.

\textbf{Why this interval, and for whom.} The learnable corridor is the gap between these two particular arms, and the choice of endpoints is what makes it a measurement rather than an arbitrary difference. The lower endpoint is the tuned bar, not LRU or an untuned default, because headroom is meaningful only above the strongest system one would deploy without new ideas; a weaker lower endpoint measures baseline weakness rather than opportunity. The upper endpoint is the warm ceiling, not the gross clairvoyant ceiling or Belady, because the quantity of interest is what a \emph{history-based} predictor could reach at equal budget: the gross ceiling credits compulsory-miss elimination that no history-based policy can perform, and Belady scores eviction rather than prefetch and at unmatched bandwidth. The interval between the tightest deployable lower bound and the tightest history-reachable upper bound, both at one byte rate, therefore isolates precisely the headroom available to a history-based learned scheduler, which is the class this paper concerns. It is a conservative measure for that class rather than a universal one: content-aware or online-updating predictors lie outside it and would only widen it, as the scope section notes.

\textbf{Why the warm ceiling, not subtraction.} The shortcut ``learnable $\approx$ gross corridor $-$ cold slice'' is wrong in both directions. It over-penalizes because the oracle's cold prefetches consumed budget a warm-only policy would redirect (on Wikipedia, 27\% of the gross oracle's useful prefetches were cold; redirected, that budget buys $+5.4$ warm points). It can under-penalize via eviction coupling. Measured here, subtraction misstates the corridor by up to 5.4 points, enough to flip a verdict. Reachability must be priced \emph{inside} the replay, at the budget. An invariant enforces it: the bar and the warm ceiling each record exactly zero cold prefetch hits by construction. (A first ``cold'' definition, ``not yet requested at issue,'' violated this: the bar scored 442{,}188 ``cold'' hits where the out-of-vocabulary definition gives zero. The invariant caught it.)

\textbf{Pre-registration.} The verdict bar, learnable corridor $\ge 8$ OHR points \emph{and} Pareto dominance at the warm ceiling, was fixed before any real-trace run. Baselines escalate in pre-registered stages (coarse Markov-1/2, then fine-$\tau$ plus Markov-3, then LSTM), and a LIVE verdict is provisional until it survives the strongest stage. The escalation only raises the bar, so survival is meaningful and death final.

\section{Results: Measurement}
Two questions decide Part~I: how large is the honestly measured corridor, and where does the inflated part of the gross number go? The first is answered per trace, the second by a warm/cold decomposition.

\textbf{Gross corridors and their deaths.} Table~\ref{tab:gatea} reports Gate A. Three points: the tuned bar is load-bearing (\texttt{cluster26} flips to dead once escalated); the LSTM never rescues a surviving corridor, so survivors are not weak-predictor artifacts; and liveness is not a family property (block yields both $+10.08$ and $-3.81$; KV yields both $+25.72$ and $-1.18$).

\textbf{The learnable corridor: 14--87\% is phantom.} Table~\ref{tab:learnable} decomposes the six gross-live traces. The three CDN/block traces with the largest gross corridors lose 51--87\% to the cold slice, because high object churn spends the clairvoyant budget on compulsory misses; the two low-churn KV traces lose only 14--20\%. Wikipedia is the instructive middle case: 52\% phantom, yet enough exploitable warm reuse that the honest corridor still clears the bar, but only once the warm ceiling re-spends the cold budget (naive subtraction leaves it at $+7.27$, a false kill). Per-request hit indicators for bar and warm ceiling are recorded on the same post-warmup requests; the corridor is the mean paired difference, resampled as 1{,}000 contiguous blocks, 10{,}000 resamples (block, not i.i.d., because hits are autocorrelated through cache state). All three surviving lower bounds clear the 8-point bar. (\texttt{cluster53}'s decomposition uses its Markov bar; against its higher LSTM bar, with the ceiling still handicapped to the smaller Markov budget, the corridor is $\ge +18.4$, so the verdict is unchanged.)

\textbf{Four inflation mechanisms.} Each is caught by a named component and exhibited by a named trace: \emph{under-tuned bar} (synthetic $+42{\to}-0.02$; \texttt{cluster26} $+12.5{\to}+10.3$), caught by family-tuning; \emph{bandwidth mismatch} (synthetic oracle at $1.27\times$), caught by the rate bucket; \emph{traffic-bought corridor} (\texttt{cluster26}, $1.16\times$ vs $1.09\times$), caught by the Pareto check; \emph{cold conflation} (\texttt{meta\_rprn} $+32.6$ gross, $+4.3$ learnable), caught by the warm restriction.

\textbf{The instrument versus its authors.} A protocol's credibility is what it catches, including from its own authors. Four self-catches are on the record, in order. A synthetic $+42$ corridor became $-0.02$ once the bar was family-tuned and the oracle bandwidth-matched. \texttt{cluster26}'s $+12.5$ became dead once fine-$\tau$ plus Markov-3 raised the bar five points and the Pareto check exposed the residual as 7\% extra traffic. A first ``cold'' definition (``not yet requested at issue'') scored 442{,}188 impossible cold hits on the \emph{bar} and was retracted for the out-of-vocabulary definition, which restores the zero-cold invariant. And Wikipedia's naive-subtraction false kill ($+7.27$, below the bar) was reversed by the warm ceiling to $+12.72$, with the 8-point bar never moving in either direction. Part II adds five further self-caught arm bugs before any conclusion, two of them in the gates' own oracle arms. A gate that never catches anything is not a gate.

\textbf{Mechanism: live vs dead.} The separation is a two-factor property. \emph{Dead:} the tuned bar already saturates the warm ceiling (\texttt{meta\_reag} 0.754 vs 0.791; \texttt{msr\_hm\_0} 0.838 vs 0.887), so what remains of the gross corridor is compulsory cold. \emph{Live:} exploitable warm reuse the tuned predictor fails to convert (Wikipedia bar 0.553 vs warm 0.680: in-vocabulary, perfectly-servable at the same budget, but fixed-threshold association cannot schedule it). The learnable corridor is exactly the quantity a learned scheduler should target.

\textbf{Eviction and prefetching are substitutes.} If better eviction and better prefetching fixed different misses, a joint policy could exploit the coupling. A $2\times2$ of $\{$S3-FIFO, Belady$\}\times\{$no prefetch, clairvoyant@matched-BW$\}$ measures the interaction (eviction gain under oracle prefetch minus eviction gain without). Six traces, all negative: \texttt{msr\_proj\_0} $-1.34$, \texttt{msr\_hm\_0} $-2.31$, wiki $-3.88$, \texttt{cluster53} $-6.66$, \texttt{cluster50} $-7.83$, \texttt{meta\_rprn} $-7.94$. Better prefetching shrinks what better eviction can add everywhere measured; the decoupled architecture is the indicated design, and this licenses freezing S3-FIFO in Part II. This result also killed the joint-RL thesis the project began with, reported as a finding rather than repurposed as motivation.

\section{The Necessity Ladder}
The three live corridors invite a learned scheduler. The Necessity Ladder replaces that reflex with a question sequence, each rung a pre-registered, replay-only gate isolating \emph{one} ingredient with an oracle and pricing it before anything is trained (Figure~\ref{fig:ladder}, Table~\ref{tab:decomp}). Every arm freezes S3-FIFO, respects the bar's byte budget through the same token bucket, and reports paired block-bootstrap CIs. Oracle arms exist only to bound what is possible.

\caption{The Necessity Ladder as run (wiki / cluster50). Green rungs are the two clairvoyant checks that certify the corridor reachable in principle (F1 timing, F5 coverage); red rungs are the kills. Each rung is a pre-registered oracle decomposition that isolates one ingredient and prices it by replay before any model is trained. The corridor survives every rung that could certify it and dies on every causal ingredient, ending with CGP, a policy built specifically to escape the bottleneck.}
\label{fig:ladder}
\end{figure}

\textbf{F1: the corridor is timing, in principle.} Replacing only issue timing with an oracle over the predictor's own vocabulary recovers 95.8\% of the learnable corridor on Wikipedia and 91.2\% on \texttt{cluster50}: the corridor is a scheduling quantity, not object choice. \texttt{cluster53} recovers only 7.3\% (its corridor is object choice, a predictor problem) and leaves the capture study; claims below hold on both remaining live traces.

\textbf{F4$'$: learned arbitration is unnecessary.} Before an RL agent earns a budget, its decision must be non-separable. A joint knapsack chooser beats a per-candidate threshold rule, given identical value estimates and budget, by $+0.01$ (wiki) and $-0.01$ (\texttt{cluster50}); the pre-registered 2-point gate fails by two orders of magnitude. A clairvoyant control reads $\approx 0$, validating the solver. Arbitration is separable; a threshold rule is the method.

\textbf{F1-stream: timing what is offered loses.} Perfect timing applied only to the candidate stream the bar actually emits ($k{=}1$), dropping never-reused candidates, lands \emph{below} the bar: $-7.01$ $[-7.68,-6.33]$ (wiki), $-10.82$ $[-11.70,-9.93]$ (\texttt{cluster50}). No scheduler can win by re-timing candidates the baseline already names; the corridor is a \emph{coverage} problem.

\textbf{F5: coverage plus clairvoyant timing reaches it.} Widening emission (fanout to 32, floor removed) and covering only \emph{causally coverable} uses (the object was emitted since its previous use) just in time clears the gate: $+10.41$ $[8.74,12.08]$ (wiki, $k{=}32$), $+11.42$ $[9.95,12.91]$ (\texttt{cluster50}, $k{=}16$). The corridor is reachable in principle; width supplies candidates, perfect insertion converts them.

\textbf{Policy 1: at-emission dispatch captures $\le 6\%$.} The causal realization fetches candidates at emission and holds them. A diagnostic arm uses a \emph{perfect} forecaster with F5's own selection, so the only difference from F5 is \emph{when} the fetch happens. It loses 10--15 points to F5 in every configuration; at-emission captures at most 6\% of the corridor, negative on \texttt{cluster50}. Since even a perfect forecaster fails at emission, no better forecaster rescues this design: the missing ingredient is just-in-time insertion.

\textbf{F7: causal timing cannot place the insertion.} F7 keeps F5's clairvoyant coverage and selection (every funded object will be used again) and swaps only the wake clock: \texttt{oracle} (true next use, the construction check) versus \texttt{model} (a causal hazard model of time-to-next-use, binned over recency/frequency/confidence, three-way split). The hazard model \emph{passes} the standard learnability gate (Spearman $0.60$/$0.43$, never-used AUC $0.92$/$0.81$), and the construction check passes ($+9.69$, 93.1\% of F5 on wiki; $+11.69$, 102.4\% on \texttt{cluster50}; both 100\% on-time). The model arm nevertheless lands at $-40.06$ $[-42.7,-37.4]$ (wiki) and $-26.01$ $[-27.9,-24.1]$ (\texttt{cluster50}), far below the bar. Every funded object is one F5 also funds, yet precision collapses to $0.05$--$0.06$ and the policy issues $1.0$--$1.5$M fetches against the bar's $1.0$M. The pre-registered fair-shot fallback retrains the hazard on the wide stream, \emph{raising} its rank correlation ($0.60{\to}0.68$, AUC $0.94$) while leaving capture unchanged ($-40.4$): a better-fit forecaster places insertions no better.

\textbf{The mechanism: endogenous slack.} Why does a hazard model that ranks lags well fail at placement? Under the bar's modest load, prefetched objects survive to use ($S(100){=}1.0$), so a timing model would seem to enjoy vast slack. But slack is a property of the policy, not the workload. A wide causal policy that mistimes issues far more fetches (precision toward 0.05), and that volume churns the cache, collapsing the survival curve, which shrinks the residence window every subsequent fetch must hit. Measured directly: under the wide load $S(100)$ falls from $1.0$ to $0.055$ (wiki) and $0.135$ (\texttt{cluster50}), median residence to 54 and 22 requests. Clairvoyant insertion needs zero slack; a log-binned hazard has multiplicative error on heavy-tailed lags, so at any hedging quantile it overshoots some uses and, arriving early on others, its own churn evicts them first. The general lesson: \textbf{rank-learnable is not placeable}. A learnability gate certifies ordering; insertion needs the signal inside a window whose width the decision itself sets. The F2-pass/F7-fail pair is, to current knowledge, the first direct demonstration that the gap can be total.

\textbf{The constructive escape, and why it also fails.} Endogenous slack has a constructive dual: buy coverage \emph{without} buying volume. Corroboration-Gated Prefetch (CGP) is the minimal such design. A wide predictor \emph{arms} candidates into a byte-free watchlist (no fetch, no cache slot); a tight predictor then \emph{fires} an insertion only on its own event, scored $s(X){=}\mathrm{conf}_{\mathrm{fire}}(X)(1{+}\beta\,\mathrm{armed}(X))$ and funded under the bar's exact byte budget. Coverage comes from the free arm; byte volume is pinned at the bar's, so the survival curve cannot collapse. CGP is separable (escapes F4$'$), fetches a set the bar never offers (F1-stream), never fetches at emission (Policy 1), and binds insertion to an event not a forecast time (F7); its clairvoyant limit is F5. It fails: $-7.60$ $[-8.1,-7.1]$ (wiki), $-5.39$ $[-5.9,-4.9]$ (\texttt{cluster50}). The reason is specific: raising $\beta$ shifts the funded set from 43\% to 69\% armed while its precision does not move (0.269 to 0.268), so the arm lift over the fire-only ablation is $+0.18$ and $-0.04$. The corroboration signal is statistically redundant with the predictor's own confidence: ``this object was plausible over a long horizon'' adds nothing to ``this object is likely next.'' There is no causal way to widen coverage without widening the low-precision volume endogenous slack punishes. This is a stronger negative than a bare failure, because the policy was designed against the exact bottleneck and the bottleneck absorbed it.

\section{A Second Domain: LLM KV-Cache Serving}
The Instrument earns its standing by returning different answers on different inputs. Applied unchanged to KV-cache warming in LLM serving on Mooncake production traces \citep{mooncake} (one hour each of conversation and tool/agent traffic; objects are 512-token KV blocks with hashed identities), warming a block into the HBM cache before its next use is the prefetch analog and OHR measures prefill compute avoided, the time-to-first-token lever. Two adaptations keep the port honest: \emph{turn gating} (a request reads its whole prefix at once, so per-access prefetch would fake-convert whole chains at zero latency; every arm may warm only at request boundaries, making the corridor measure cross-turn warming), and a \emph{physical} reachability restriction (a KV block never computed cannot be warmed by any system, so the warm ceiling is the true ceiling). The bars are inherited unchanged; a BAR-CAP control (bar under the ceiling's bucket) separates burst-versus-bucket protocol cost from scheduling quality.

The verdict (Table~\ref{tab:llm}) is the third distinct outcome: \textbf{no corridor}. A tuned Markov table over block IDs already sits within noise of the physically reachable, bandwidth-matched clairvoyant ceiling; even like-for-like the margin is $+2$--$3$ points against an 8-point gate. The reason is structural: prefix-chain reuse is near-deterministic (held-out next-request coverage 0.728 vs 0.008 for a popularity baseline), so the trivial policy is already the ceiling. The conversation trace's reuse \emph{timing} is learnable (think-time hazard $\rho{=}0.27$, AUC 0.81), but there is no corridor for that signal to capture; tool/agent has neither ($\rho{=}0.003$). Anyone about to train an ML KV-cache warmer would be told by one replay: do not. Table~\ref{tab:three} collects the three-domain verdict.

\section{Scope and Threats to Validity}
Five limits bound the claims, and naming them also fixes their direction: the measurement caveats point toward conservatism, the capture caveats toward a narrower but firmer kill.

``Learnable'' is defined against frozen, history-based prediction (training-prefix vocabulary). An online-updating or content-aware predictor could reach cold objects and would \emph{raise} live corridors, so the measurement verdicts are conservative in that direction. The capture verdict is not symmetric: F5 already grants the widest emission the family supports, and F7/CGP fail for timing and redundancy reasons a larger vocabulary does not touch; the F7 kill was accepted only after the pre-registered wide-stream fallback. The capture kill is bounded by its decomposition (Table~\ref{tab:decomp}); a design outside it (for example, modifying the evictor to protect warmed objects) is out of scope, though the substitutes result argues against it. One cache size (1\%), 2M-request prefixes, request-weighted OHR; byte-weighted results and cache-size sweeps are future work. Twitter traces are 1-in-10 samples, which stretches inter-reference gaps, so absolute OHRs are not comparable to unsampled deployments. LLM traces cover one hour of one provider, and lags are in access-index units. CIs cover within-trace variance only; the escalation protocol addresses sweep granularity structurally, since every error in the bar inflates corridors and the capture kills are upper bounds on scrutiny.

\section{Conclusion}
Measured honestly, against a family-tuned baseline, at matched bandwidth, above a ceiling restricted to reachable objects, most celebrated prefetch headroom in caching is phantom: 14--87\% of gross corridors evaporate across 12 production traces, and the biggest corridors lose the most. What survives is real and bootstrap-solid, concentrated where in-vocabulary reuse exists but fixed-threshold prediction mistimes it. A ladder of pre-registered oracle decompositions then prices each ingredient a learned scheduler could contribute: learned arbitration adds under a point, perfect timing of the offered candidates loses, wide coverage with clairvoyant insertion reaches the corridor but fetching at prediction time forfeits it, causal insertion timing fails, and a policy built specifically to buy coverage without volume fails last, because its corroboration signal is redundant with the predictor's own confidence. The reason has a name, endogenous slack, and a general form: rank-learnable is not placeable. The corridor that survives the honest ruler is priced in clairvoyance. The Instrument's value is that it discriminates: no corridor on block storage, a clairvoyance-priced corridor on CDN and KV, and no corridor on LLM KV-cache serving where a tuned table already sits on the physical ceiling. Three families, three verdicts, three mechanisms, each established by replay before a single model was trained, and each a specific answer to the question every learned-systems paper should ask first: is there anything here for learning to win? The gates caught their own authors nine times before judging anyone else; that trail, more than any single number, is the argument for adopting them. The instrument, the ladder, and all replay code are released so the next celebrated headroom number can be checked in an afternoon.
